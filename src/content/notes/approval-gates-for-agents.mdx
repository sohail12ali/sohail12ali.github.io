The fastest way to lose trust in a coding agent is to let it do one irreversible thing you didn't expect.

## Gate the effects, not the thinking
Agents can read, plan and draft as much as they want. The gates go on **effects**: shell commands, writes outside the workspace, deploys. In Mothership, a `PreToolUse` hook holds those calls and the console asks:

- **Allow once**
- **Allow for this chat**
- **Deny**

If nobody answers within 300 seconds, the call is denied. Silence is never approval.

## Gate the claims too
"Done" is also a claim that needs checking. The verifier won't close a ticket until every acceptance criterion has **cited evidence**, such as a test run, a screenshot or a log line.

## Don't become the bottleneck
- Group decisions so you approve a plan once, not every step.
- Make the safe path the default, so most work never needs a gate.
- Use voice. The harness says out loud when it needs you, so you don't have to keep watching it.
